Air Fryer Chicken Recipes

Ten tested air fryer chicken recipes with exact times and temperatures, from crispy thighs to parmesan-crusted breasts. Each recipe is timed for real basket air fryers.

Chicken is the single most-asked-about ingredient for air fryers, and for good reason. The combination of rapid hot-air circulation and a compact cooking chamber produces the kind of crisp skin normally reserved for deep frying — with a fraction of the oil, in about two-thirds the time of a conventional oven. Once you learn a few rules about temperature, moisture, and timing, chicken in an air fryer becomes one of the most reliable weeknight proteins you can cook.

The ten recipes in this collection have all been developed specifically for basket-style air fryers in the 4- to 6-quart range (the most common size sold today). They cover the whole spectrum: bone-in thighs and drumsticks for dinner, wings for snacks and game days, tenders for kids and quick lunches, and stuffed or crusted breast preparations when you want something more substantial. Times and temperatures are calibrated for the cuts as purchased at supermarkets in the US, UK, and EU — meaning 150–180 g thighs, 80–100 g drumsticks, and standard broiler-size wings.

The three rules that make air fryer chicken work

Start with a dry surface. This is the single biggest variable in whether chicken comes out crisp or leathery. Raw chicken releases a surprising amount of moisture while it cooks, and if the surface is already wet when it enters the air fryer, browning can't start until that water evaporates. By then the interior is overcooked. Pat pieces dry with paper towels, then let them sit uncovered on a plate for 10 minutes after seasoning — or for wings and bone-in pieces, leave them in the fridge uncovered for a couple of hours. The surface visibly darkens as it dries, and this is exactly what you want.

Don't crowd the basket. Hot air has to reach every surface. Piling pieces means the ones on the bottom steam rather than crisp. A 4-quart basket comfortably fits about 500 g of thighs or drumsticks in a single layer with small gaps between pieces. If you need to cook more, work in batches — the second batch cooks faster than the first because the chamber is already hot.

Use a thermometer, not the clock. Chicken timing varies with piece size, starting temperature (fridge-cold vs. room temperature), and the specific air fryer model. An instant-read thermometer removes the guesswork: white meat is done at 74 °C (165 °F), thighs and drumsticks can safely go to 80–82 °C (175–180 °F) where the connective tissue breaks down and the texture improves. A cheap probe thermometer pays for itself within a week of air-fryer cooking.

Cut-by-cut timing reference

  • Wings: 200 °C (400 °F), 22–25 minutes, flipping once. Toss in baking powder beforehand for extra crispness.
  • Drumsticks: 190 °C (375 °F), 22–26 minutes. Turn halfway through.
  • Bone-in thighs: 200 °C (400 °F), 18–22 minutes, skin side up for the final 10 minutes.
  • Boneless thighs: 200 °C (400 °F), 14–18 minutes. Faster and more forgiving than breasts.
  • Tenders: 200 °C (400 °F), 8–10 minutes, flipping once. Breaded versions need a light oil spritz.
  • Boneless breast (halved horizontally): 190 °C (375 °F), 10–12 minutes. Brine for 15 minutes first to prevent dryness.

Sauces and glazes — when to add them

Sugar-based sauces (honey, teriyaki, BBQ) burn at air-fryer temperatures, so they can't go on at the start. The safe approach is to cook the chicken almost fully, then brush or toss in sauce for the final 2–3 minutes. This is especially true for anything with a sweet-chili, honey-garlic, or sticky Asian-style glaze. For dry spice rubs, you can apply them before cooking — they don't burn the same way.

Frequently asked questions

What's the best cut of chicken for an air fryer?
Bone-in thighs are the most forgiving — they stay juicy even if slightly overcooked, develop excellent crisp skin, and take just 18–22 minutes. For quick cooking, boneless thighs or tenders are the fastest (14–18 minutes and 8–10 minutes respectively). Boneless chicken breast is the trickiest because it dries out fast; halving it horizontally and brining first makes a noticeable difference.
Do I need to preheat my air fryer for chicken?
Yes, especially for pieces where you want crisp skin. A 2–3 minute preheat at the cooking temperature means the surface starts browning immediately rather than slowly warming up. Some recent models have a dedicated preheat mode; for the rest, just run the empty basket for a few minutes before adding food.
How do I keep chicken from sticking to the air fryer basket?
Perforated parchment liners are the cleanest solution for breaded pieces and anything delicate. For whole cuts, a light spritz of oil on both the basket and the chicken skin prevents sticking. Don't use aerosol cooking sprays — the propellants damage the non-stick coating over time.
Can I cook frozen chicken in an air fryer?
Frozen tenders and nuggets cook well directly from the freezer at 200 °C — add 3–5 minutes to the package time. Whole cuts (breasts, thighs, drumsticks) technically work but cook unevenly: the outside overcooks before the inside hits 74 °C. Thawing first produces significantly better texture.
How much oil do I actually need for air fryer chicken?
Less than you'd think. Skin-on pieces render their own fat as they cook, so a light spritz or brushing at the start is enough. Breaded pieces benefit from a thin spritz on both sides to help the coating crisp. Boneless, skinless cuts (tenders, breast) need a slightly more generous coat of oil to prevent drying out — about a teaspoon per 400 g of chicken.
Why did my air fryer chicken come out rubbery instead of crisp?
Almost always one of three causes: moisture on the surface before cooking (dry thoroughly with paper towels), the basket was too crowded (cook in batches), or the temperature was too low (most crisp targets want 190–200 °C, not 160–180 °C). Chicken skin specifically also benefits from sitting uncovered in the fridge for a couple of hours beforehand.
